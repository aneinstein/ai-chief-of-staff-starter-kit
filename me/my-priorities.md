# My Priorities — Strategic Focus Workbook

This file helps your AI understand what matters most to you right now — so it can filter the noise and surface what's actually important. Your briefing, meeting prep, and weekly review skills all read this.

**How to use this:** Work through the discovery questions once, then update this file monthly (or whenever your priorities shift). This is a living document, not a one-time exercise.

---

## The Big Picture

> **Why this matters:** If your AI doesn't know what you're trying to accomplish, it treats everything as equally important. That's the opposite of helpful. The whole point of a chief of staff is to filter — and filtering requires knowing what you care about.

### Discovery Questions

Before writing anything, spend 5 minutes thinking about these:

1. **If your quarter ended tomorrow**, what 2-3 things would you most want to have made progress on?
2. **What's the thing that only you can move forward?** The thing that stalls if you don't personally push it?
3. **What keeps falling through the cracks?** What do you intend to do but never actually get to?
4. **What would your manager say your top priority should be?** Is that the same as what you'd say?
5. **What are you spending time on that someone else could handle?** Where are you the bottleneck unnecessarily?

---

## My Top Priorities

List 3-5 priorities. For each one, capture:

- **What it is** — in plain language
- **Why it matters** — what happens if this succeeds? What happens if you ignore it?
- **Why only you** — what about this requires your specific involvement?
- **This week's move** — what's the concrete next action? (This keeps it grounded and actionable.)

### Priority Template

```markdown
### [Priority Name]

**What:** [One sentence — what are you trying to accomplish?]

**Why it matters:** [What's at stake? What does success look like?]

**Why only me:** [Why can't this be delegated? What do you uniquely bring?]

**This week's move:** [One concrete action you can take this week]

**Status:** [On track / Needs attention / Stalled / Blocked by ___]
```

### Worked Examples

These show different levels of seniority and different types of priorities:

**Executive example:**
```
### Launch enterprise tier

**What:** Ship our enterprise product tier by end of Q1 with pricing, packaging,
and 3 signed design partners.

**Why it matters:** Company revenue depends on moving upmarket. Board expects
$2M ARR from enterprise by year-end. This quarter's launch is the foundation.

**Why only me:** I own the product decision on what's included in enterprise
vs. pro tier. Engineering can build, sales can sell, but the packaging
decision is mine.

**This week's move:** Review the pricing model the team drafted and give feedback
by Wednesday.

**Status:** On track but tight. Design partner #3 still not confirmed.
```

**Manager example:**
```
### Reduce team oncall burden

**What:** Get the team's oncall pages down from 15/week to under 5 by improving
alerting and fixing the top 3 noisy services.

**Why it matters:** The team is burning out. Two people have mentioned oncall
in their 1:1s as a reason they're considering other roles.

**Why only me:** I need to negotiate with platform team for shared oncall
coverage, which requires manager-to-manager alignment. I also need to
approve the engineering investment (2 sprints) to fix the alerting.

**This week's move:** Meet with platform team lead to discuss shared coverage
model. Bring data on page frequency by service.

**Status:** Needs attention — haven't scheduled the meeting yet.
```

**IC example:**
```
### Publish the recommendation engine analysis

**What:** Complete the analysis comparing our current rec engine vs. the new
ML model and present findings to the product council.

**Why it matters:** The product team is about to decide whether to invest a
quarter in rebuilding the engine. My analysis is the key input to that decision.

**Why only me:** I'm the only person who understands both the current system
and the new model well enough to do an honest comparison.

**This week's move:** Finish the A/B test analysis and draft slides 1-5.

**Status:** On track. A/B results look promising but I need another week of data.
```

## What I'm Delegating (or Should Be)

> **Why this matters:** One of the most powerful things an AI chief of staff can do is remind you what you've delegated and whether it's on track. But it needs to know what you've handed off.

**Think about:**
- What have you asked someone else to do that you haven't heard back on?
- What are you still doing yourself that someone else could handle?
- What would you delegate if you trusted someone to do it well?

### Delegation Template

```markdown
### [Task/Project]
**Delegated to:** [Name]
**Expected by:** [Date or milestone]
**Last update:** [When did you last hear about this?]
**My role:** [Am I truly hands-off, or do I need to review/approve?]
```

## What I'm Saying No To

> **Why this matters:** Priorities only work if you also know what you're NOT doing. Listing these explicitly helps your AI stop surfacing things you've consciously deprioritized.

**Think about:**
- What requests have you turned down this quarter?
- What "good ideas" are you choosing not to pursue right now?
- What standing meetings or projects are you thinking about dropping?

## Revisiting This File

**Monthly (minimum):** Update "this week's move" and "status" for each priority. Add or remove priorities as things change.

**Quarterly:** Do a full rewrite. Your priorities should meaningfully change each quarter. If they don't, either you're not being strategic about your time or your priorities are too vague.

**After a major shift:** New role, reorg, new quarter, new project — update immediately. Stale priorities are worse than no priorities because they make your AI confidently focus on the wrong things.
